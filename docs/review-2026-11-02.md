## Overall assessment

The native product foundation matches much of the handoff: generated scenes rather than preset practice rooms, a real stereo renderer, local Apple speech, freeform intake, explicit answer completion, shared pause-aware movement/countdown, optional reflection and grounded feedback, durable records/CSV, and adapted repeats. Registered assets and declarative operations are sensible boundaries.

The largest product drift is that practicing a situation has become progressing through a linear sequence of spoken instructions. The largest construction weakness is that scene authoring jumps from a prose brief to final positions and operations without a validated spatial/temporal plan. Additional effects will help appearance, but cannot by themselves correct staging, support heights, blocked routes, or missing activities.

Apple on-device speech, explicit intake completion/readback, and the expanded cumulative $50 cap are documented later user decisions. They should not be treated as regressions merely because the original handoff describes Meta transcription or a simpler intake flow.

## Prioritized findings

### 1. P1 — Accepted audio output can crash the phone

`backend/src/contracts.ts:57` collects audio cue IDs into a Set but never rejects duplicates. AudioPlan's schema also accepts two layers with the same ID. `ios/Braive/Audio.swift:172` builds `Dictionary(uniqueKeysWithValues:)` from those IDs; duplicate keys are a Swift runtime precondition failure, outside the recoverable generation repair path.

Evidence: the duplicate-ID probe passes both AudioPlan schema validation and `sceneIssues` with zero issues. This establishes an accepted input reaching a trapping native operation, not a reproduced physical-phone crash.

Fix direction: enforce unique layer IDs before accepting audio and defensively reject them on the phone before dictionary construction. Apply reference and schedule checks to audio as a first-class artifact.

### 2. P1 — A second machine can accidentally create another full spending allowance

`scripts/start-phone-test.mjs:6` initializes a fresh $50 ledger when its local ignored ledger does not exist. This checkout has neither `backend/.env` nor `backend/data/meta-test-approval.json`. A fresh clone therefore has no knowledge of spending on the Mac. The existing lock protects one filesystem ledger; it does not coordinate independent devices or copied ledgers.

This conflicts with the documented single cumulative allowance across processes/chats. Nothing was launched against live providers here and no new ledger was initialized.

Fix direction: use the existing Mac backend as the single spending authority, or explicitly reconcile and transfer authority while other billable processes are stopped. Do not simply copy an old ledger and allow both copies to run. A continuation launcher should fail closed when the authoritative ledger is missing.

### 3. P2 — Validated automatic progression can depend on an event that cannot happen yet

`backend/src/contracts.ts:103` verifies that a completion path is executed somewhere in the entire scene, not that it can execute before completion of the current stage. The probe gives the first stage no actions and makes it wait for `glide_path`, launched only in a later stage. It passes schema/cross-field validation. `ScenarioRuntime.tick` can never see that completion in the first stage, so progress stalls until the global countdown ends.

Fix direction: validate temporal reachability and dependencies, including inherited active motion, mutually exclusive branches, and competing movement on the same actor. Require automatic stage exits to have a reachable cause. This matters more as the partner makes progression automatic.

### 4. P2 — Entering the final stage is treated as completing its activity

`ios/Braive/Session.swift:703` marks completion upon entry to any terminal step. At line 684 it finishes after three active seconds once speech is quiet, without waiting for final-stage movement or task conditions. A final stage starting a ten-second path is accepted by the validators and can be cut off after roughly three seconds. Speech may delay that cutoff; movement does not.

This also permits a practice to finish well before the coordinator's chosen duration and record it as completed. Whether early task completion should be allowed is a product decision; completing the final activity must be distinct from merely entering its stage.

Fix direction: explicit task completion conditions and a clear policy for remaining practice time. Test a terminal landing/arrival with a longer path, rather than only a terminal message.

### 5. P2 — Preflight does not validate the world the participant actually enters

`ios/Braive/World.swift:248` runs preflight on the unmodified node graph. First-stage `start_operations` run later through `Session.applyScenario`, and scene events run after active practice begins. The focus can pass preflight and be hidden at time zero. The corresponding backend probe is accepted.

`ScenePreflight.swift:52–78` replaces source materials with opaque mask materials, dropping original opacity and double-sided settings. Transparent water/glass and thin surfaces can therefore have different occlusion behavior in the check and the actual view. This is a source-level finding; no new GPU comparison was run here.

Movement paths are validated for IDs, increasing times, and some displacement, but there is no geometric swept-clearance/support test for a participant or actor along those paths. An opening render does not establish a usable route through the scene.

Fix direction: validate an isolated initial-state snapshot after initial operations, preserve material visibility semantics in masks, and sample relevant stage/path states for support, collisions, clearance and task visibility. Preserve the existing both-eye checks.

### 6. P2 — Several advertised renderer capabilities are not enforced or consumed

`backend/src/server.ts:44` advertises 700 draw calls, 16 MB texture bytes, and 180 MB resident memory. `World.swift:265–279` enforces resolved nodes, primitive counts, and source geometry bytes, not those other budgets. It does not measure actual draw calls or resident memory. These are declared targets, not measured guarantees.

Material `texture_asset_id` exists in OpenAPI but `World.material` does not read it. For loaded GLBs, the initial authored Material is generally ignored except for registered character tint; a prompt can request a property the renderer never applies. `viewpoint_transition` promises a fade but immediately relocates the viewer. SceneSpec's separate reveal timings are ignored in favor of one hardcoded 1.5-second reveal.

Fix direction: make the capability manifest reflect executable behavior, implement or remove unsupported generated fields, and measure representative device costs before adding effects. Keep authored asset materials intact by default, with explicit supported overrides.

### 7. P2 — Feedback can arrive after the user has moved on

`Session.feedback` at line 803 starts an untracked Task. The app accepts CONTINUE while that request is pending, allowing save and repeat navigation. When the old request returns, it writes the shared `record`, changes the shared message, and speaks without checking session ID, phase, revision or a request nonce. It can speak over a later screen or write old feedback into a newer record.

Dialogue has cancellation/nonces and post-request revision checks; feedback lacks equivalent ownership. Backend feedback/adaptation routes verify cited evidence IDs but do not cross-check returned routing fields against the requested plan.

Fix direction: bind asynchronous post-session work to a session and phase, and cancel or discard stale responses. Either wait for feedback before offering Continue or define what skipping a pending request means. Verify with a delayed fake response and a rapid continue/repeat sequence.

### 8. Product deviation — The interaction contract overconstrains natural experiences

`ScenarioRuntime` permits transitions only to the next approved stage. `Session.perform` ignores ordinary practice speech unless it matches a local action or begins ASK/TALK TO. Action experiences must disable conversation entirely (`contracts.ts:111`), including an activity where a participant might naturally ask a guide something while moving.

The explicit routing was a deliberate response to slow/unwanted character replies, so this is not a request to restore a network response to every utterance. It does mean the current build does not deliver the earlier demo-script flow of simply giving a proposal and receiving an audience follow-up. Branching navigation also collapses into a strictly ordered chain rather than meaningful alternative routes.

The partner is changing automatic progression, so this is a coordination item rather than a competing implementation. Desired distinction: automatic world events, genuine participant decisions, local emergency controls, and optional conversation should have separate triggers. Instructions should appear when the person needs a decision or help, rather than every time the world changes.

### 9. P2 — Audio identities and time semantics are inconsistent

Audio layers have unique conceptual identities, but `Audio.players` is keyed by asset filename. Two distinct layers using the same wind/room asset overwrite one another. Ending one layer stops the player used by another; a one-shot cue can also replace an ambient loop. Layer positions are not used for spatial playback.

The reversed-time probe (`start=30`, `end=10`) passes validation. Duplicate cue IDs and schedule coherence deserve validation before rendering, not only the scene's references to cues.

Fix direction: key scheduled players by layer ID, isolate transient cues, validate start/end/ramp bounds against the practice duration, and state whether spatial audio is actually supported.

## Additional inconsistencies and limits

- The full handoff treats model tool calls as executable typed scheduling instructions. The server validates their phases but uses a fixed generation pipeline; it does not dispatch according to those calls. Keep either a truthful fixed pipeline contract or an actual dispatcher, rather than redundant planning output.
- OpenAPI still lists internal `/v1/scenes`, `/v1/audio-plans` and remote `/v1/transcription-sessions` endpoints that are not exposed by this server. The implementation intentionally keeps specialists internal and uses local speech. Conversely, native validation and provenance extensions are not described as paths there. Separate the current wire API from internal artifact schemas and historical interfaces.
- SceneSpec allows 500 authored nodes while the server/native loader cap is 350. New-provider required fields are added dynamically by `outputSchema`; the base schemas/types intentionally allow legacy records. That migration distinction needs an explicit documented contract rather than the claim that all three shapes are identical.
- `Audio.fitIntro` falls back to “Practice your opening” for any scene whose introduction is too long, which is speech-specific copy in a freeform action product. Its fallback measurement result is not checked again. Retain the requested situation in a bounded intro.
- Destination preloaders mostly show the next prompt for 0.45 seconds; the full destination-specific tip sequence in the handoff is not implemented. Several exact history/STOP prompts have been rewritten. These are lower-priority copy/flow deviations, not reasons to interrupt the partner's runtime work.
- The durable session record contains the plan and observations but not the actual generated SceneSpec/AudioPlan. Crash recovery converts the initial running record to interrupted, but active progress is not periodically saved. Do not imply that the local record can reconstruct the complete generated experience or recover all unsaved observations after a crash.
- `docs/asset-library.md` still says all characters must use the neutral base and props must be GLBs, conflicting with clothed character selection and approved procedural assemblies. `docs/demo-walkthrough.md` still mentions the original $5 allowance. The demo's conversational sequence predates the explicit ASK routing. Preserve chronology in verification records, but update current instructions.
- This audit has not established optical comfort, correct landscape-left tracking, real microphone echo behavior, new-movement smoothness, visual quality across varied requests, or sustained device frame/memory performance. Historical phone results belong to the versions noted in the verification log.

## Proposed next phase: spatial planning and visual quality

This is a proposed design, not an implemented schema migration. It should be integrated after reconciling the partner's automatic-scene changes.

### Borrow useful structure from proven systems

1. **Constraint-based scene layout:** [Holodeck, CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Yang_Holodeck_Language_Guided_Generation_of_3D_Embodied_AI_Environments_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf) uses generated spatial relations and layout optimization. Its layout comparison supports relations over directly asking a language model for every absolute position. For Braive, represent on/inside/facing/near/clear-of relations and support anchors, then resolve coordinates deterministically using real asset bounds. Its published results are not proof of phone VR quality here.
2. **Hierarchical task planning:** [SHOP/M-SHOP](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/a777fa5a-2c64-4ead-ba63-5f1497fcbe85) and [SHOP2](https://research.ibm.com/publications/shop2-an-htn-planning-system) decompose tasks while tracking the world state through execution. Borrow objective → subtasks → executable effects, with preconditions and completion conditions. A construction order is not a requirement for the participant to say a command at each step.
3. **Construction information requirements:** [buildingSMART IDS](https://github.com/buildingSMART/IDS/blob/development/Documentation/UserManual/README.md) separates applicability from requirements so model information can be checked. Borrow that separation for “every seated actor has a compatible support and clearance” or “every traversed route has a usable swept volume.” IDS itself checks IFC information; adopting it would not provide a geometric collision solver. Braive need not adopt IFC/XML.

These are design inferences from the sources, not drop-in integrations or claims that more prompts alone solve geometry.

### Keep a compact generation pipeline

Use the existing coordinator/authoring calls to produce a structured brief and an assembly plan, then lower that plan into native scene data. Avoid adding a long sequence of model calls that consumes the 120-second budget.

- Experience intent: requested situation, boundaries, measurable task outcomes, necessary participants, genuine decisions, automatic events, optional assistance.
- Spatial plan: zones, dimensions, supports/anchors, local assembly hierarchies, asset choices and measured bounds, route corridors, sightlines, key task objects, and near/middle/far composition.
- Temporal plan: entry conditions, effects, occupied actor/path resources, event dependencies, completion conditions, and terminal success independent of stage entry.
- Visual plan: sky/environment, palette, sun/key light, fog range, surface profiles, and a bounded effect selection. Decorative detail has a lower priority than task realization.
- Compiler/validator: stable application-owned IDs, transform resolution, support/clearance checks, executable operations, actual resource accounting, both-eye state checks, and precise failures for the existing one repair allowance.

Keep authored intermediate construction data separate from the public wire API where possible. Add only fields with a native implementation, validation, and a record-version strategy. Test equivalent representations across backend schema, TypeScript and Swift.

### Skyboxes and shaders

The current background is a solid color plus fog. The production Metal shader performs stereo compositing/lens correction; the other shader snippets are diagnostic visibility masks, not scene surface effects.

- Start with an app-owned procedural sky or licensed panorama/cubemap, parameterized by horizon/zenith color, sun direction, haze and time of day. Keep nearby task geometry and meaningful stereo depth. A skybox supplies distance context, not the task world.
- Add a small allowlist of native surface profiles for useful materials such as ground, water, glass and foliage. Models select profile IDs and bounded parameters; shader source stays in the app.
- Drive animated surface effects with the pause-aware clock. Keep each eye's environment/effects consistent and avoid extra camera motion.
- Decouple fog distances from far-clip distance: the current minimum 150m clip and fixed 5m fog start make one scalar density a poor control for both a tight interior and a large outdoor scene.
- Verify silhouettes, horizon, grounding/contact, transparent surfaces and task visibility before adding expensive screen effects. The current directional shadows and ambient occlusion already need sustained device measurements.

### Validation for the next implementation

Use a few contrasting freeform cases: a seated interview with a desk, a crowded speaking space, a trail with an actual route decision, a small enclosure, and an action scene involving water or altitude. Check automatic progression, remaining participant agency, supported objects, clear routes, visibility through transparency, and STOP/NO during movement/effects.

Capture initial and relevant later stage views from both eyes and a diagnostic overhead view; record failures and actual resource/time measurements. Distinguish deterministic/offline replay from fresh generation. Prefer a small set of adversarial contract checks over adding a large testing framework.
