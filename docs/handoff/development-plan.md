# Generative VR resilience practice — complete development handoff

## Authority and delivery

Implement the entire team vision in the hackathon build: a native iPhone, voice-first stereographic VR app that creates timed practice situations from a person's described fear and current ability to handle it. The purpose is level-headed critical thinking and problem solving in uncomfortable or high-stakes situations, including public speaking, job interviews, getting lost on a trail, and being trapped in tight spaces. This is situational practice, not a mental-health adviser or a claim of clinical treatment.

The user-supplied ambitious-v1 addendum is authoritative for clarifications (corrected Spark role, reflection time format, and generation ceiling). The original team document governs product behavior over engineering translations. The user's corrections are binding: practice environments are generated, not preset; examples illustrate quality rather than limit supported situations; no deferred core features, stretch-scope labels, milestone gates, or narrow substitute product. The preset forest in the original document is the lobby, not a practice scene, and remains required.

This document incorporates every section of the team PDF. `original-framework.txt` is the extracted source, retained for exact wording. `requirements-map.md` maps it to implementation requirements. `openapi.json` defines the proposed project-owned interface and model-output schemas; it is not Meta's API specification. Implementation recommendations below fill gaps but must not override explicit team behavior. Numeric limits in schemas are initial engineering settings, not team-imposed creative restrictions.

Build the full loop with shared contracts, native platform services, reusable rendering capabilities, and immediate integration. Use short checks of the actual phone experience, not extensive testing infrastructure. Resolve concrete API/hardware blockers during implementation and report the closest faithful solution rather than quietly deleting capabilities. The background pack reports Indie / Build for Your World and submission Nov 4 at 9 PM ET; verify official rules, deadline, model access, and submission artifacts rather than treating participant notes as official rules.

## Complete interaction specification

### Global presentation and controls

Operation is primarily audible commands and feedback, with readable stereo text and accessible backup controls. Every page transition goes through a dedicated simple preloader with tips chosen for the destination and a whooshing wind sound. Keep the loader lightweight; show it even when assets are ready without imposing a long artificial wait. Initial launch also has the tips preloader. Pausing is an overlay, not a page transition; it must happen immediately without waiting for loading or transition audio.

Portrait orientation pauses the app until landscape returns. Preserve the prior phase, timer, narration position, dialogue, and scene event state. Returning to landscape clears the portrait pause and restores the prior phase automatically if no other pause reason remains. It never dismisses a STOP confirmation or an interruption that still needs recovery. Detect physical orientation using motion data if UI orientation is locked; face-up/unknown orientation must not generate repeated toggles.

Destination tips: lobby—START and headset placement; intake—describe the situation and current ability; scene setup—first always “To stop at any time, just say STOP!”, then “hey builder” and headset tips; reflection—optional spoken reflection; feedback—optional feedback; save/repeat—history and repeat instructions. Do not replay transition whooshes on every rotating tip. Duck background audio beneath narration and voice input; keep both lobby audio layers audible.

### 1. Launch and lobby

Show a 3D preset forest parallax scene with real depth, foreground/background separation, and atmospheric fade into the distance. Play royalty-free piano alongside forest/wind ambience. Display contrasting text: “When you’re ready to start, say START!” Reuse this screen after the user finishes rather than creating a different home flow. Stereo eye separation and rotational head response should convey depth without artificial camera movement.

### 2. Headset setup and voice confirmation

Check microphone/audio readiness and landscape orientation. In portrait say: “It looks like you’re in portrait mode! Make sure you’ve got your headset on before you start a simulation!” Get spoken confirmation to start. Orientation is a useful setup cue, not proof that the headset is worn. Obtain platform microphone permissions before insertion when possible; test audible STOP and provide a usable viewer/controller input or easy remove-viewer exit path. Keep the product native iPhone, not a web replacement.

### 3. Describe the situation and ability

Ask the user to describe their fear/situation and current ability to handle it. Do not replace this with a fixed scenario menu. Clarify only missing information necessary to produce a meaningful scene; respect any user-stated boundaries or preferences. The coordinator chooses intensity and timer from this information and relevant history. Do not force the user to choose a duration or impose an extra approval screen on every normal run. A brief audible summary may invite corrections without blocking the original flow.

Look for relevant information in the app's SESSIONS spreadsheet. If found, ask: “I see we’ve explored this situation before! Would you like to consider past sessions for this simulation?” YES includes relevant rows; NO excludes previous sessions. For a chained repeat from the end-of-session question, do not ask again: use the just-completed session and the history selection already established for that chain. Unrelated older history declined earlier stays excluded. Current user input overrides past preferences.

### 4. Coordinate and construct

Send the coordinator a prompt containing app context, the user's input/current ability, authorized relevant history, model goals, ideal output examples, and the complete available tool/capability suite. Obtain a structured plan through the project OpenAPI/schema contracts: intensity, timer, setting brief, props, narrator beats, and setting/sound/feedback tool calls. Give specialist models their own schemas and the same plan ID/revision so setting, sound, and interactions remain coherent.

Once the coordinator decides, keep those decisions stable unless further user input arrives. Do not let background models independently escalate difficulty, change the task, or extend the timer. Dialogue and authored scene events proceed within that stable plan.

Narrate: “I’ll get the scene ready. If there’s anything additional you’d like to say, just say ‘hey builder!’ and let me know!” During generation the preloader's first tip is always “To stop at any time, just say STOP!” Target around one minute average, with a hard two-minute ceiling per generation attempt as clarified in the addendum; these are requirements to implement, not demonstrated provider guarantees. Keep progress honest and let users stop while waiting. At 120 seconds cancel/invalidate unfinished generation and offer retry/revise/cancel; do not continue that attempt or silently substitute a preset. Include coordinator, specialists, repair, and required asset readiness in this attempt budget. A complete builder addition starts a new revision/attempt; do not reset the deadline for internal retries. Make restarted setup visible so repeated revisions cannot hide elapsed waiting.

“Hey builder” opens a listening turn for additional input. Support repeated additions, not just one revision. Merge each complete addition, increment the plan revision, cancel/supersede stale work, and rebuild affected parts coherently. Extension beyond the Doc: if supporting hey builder during active practice, pause, collect input, revise, then transition back into the revised scene. The required Doc behavior is hey builder during scene setup; this extension must not replace or complicate that path.

### 5. Introduction and timed experience

Begin on a black screen. Narrator describes the situation within an eight-second intro and includes the STOP reminder. Use actual synthesized-speech duration to fit concise copy; rewrite or use a short template rather than cutting off words. After the intro, fade black away while the scene is initially blurry, then resolve to clear focus. Implement blur as a brief render effect without eye-camera changes. Proposed configurable reveal duration: 1–2 seconds. Start the simulation countdown after the reveal so setup does not consume practice time.

The coordinator selects the countdown duration. Render the remaining time legibly without overwhelming the scene. Timed beats, character interactions, sounds, and user choices all share the active-session clock. Provide interactive conversations for human-interaction situations, not only prerecorded monologues. Situational navigation uses voice choices and supported scene updates while the user remains physically stationary in the viewer.

### 6. STOP, portrait, and other interruptions

Saying “STOP!” at any time pauses everything immediately: countdown, scene actions, dialogue generation/playback, narration, and ambient/SFX playback. Show a black-tinted overlay with white text: “Quit simulation?” Say the question aloud. YES skips the remaining simulation and proceeds to self-report/reflection. NO removes the STOP pause and resumes the exact prior phase once other pause reasons are clear. Silence/ambiguous speech leaves the prompt open. The recognizer and confirmation audio remain available while scene media are paused.

During setup/intro, STOP has the same confirmation semantics; NO resumes that phase. A direct backup exit may end immediately. Repeated STOP must not stack confirmation dialogs. “No” means resume only when answering this question; it must not accidentally answer history consent or feedback prompts. When the timer expires while already paused, no completion occurs until the remaining active time is consumed.

### 7. Self-report and optional reflection

After completion or user termination, ask for self-report of the experience. Recommended concise fields: perceived difficulty and current readiness, each optional 0–10, plus a freeform response. Missing ratings are null, not zero. Offer an optional vocal reflection, transcribe it, and save in the app folder's `Reflections` directory.

Preserve the team's filename convention as the visible name: `[DATE] [HH:MM] - [SIMULATION TOPIC (<=30 characters)]`. The addendum explicitly corrects the Doc’s HH::MM typo to HH:MM. Use a local-date/time display and store UTC timestamps separately. The document leaves date format unspecified; use YYYY-MM-DD. For portable exported filenames, replace colon separators with hyphens and sanitize path characters; retain the exact display title in metadata. Append a short unique suffix only on collision. Do not silently replace the named reflection file with a database-only entry.

### 8. Optional agent feedback

Ask: “Would you like to hear feedback on your experience?” On YES, generate structured agent feedback and speak/display it under:

- “Here’s what you did well”: at most three positive items.
- “I also noticed…”: at most three items, each positive or negative.
- If relevant, include at least one smaller thing the user can fix quickly so the initial difficulty does not feel insurmountable. Attach it to an observation or the short next-step field.

Use actual dialogue, actions, timings, and self-report as evidence. Distinguish self-report from observed behavior. No invented praise, eye-contact claims from head orientation, diagnosis, employability scoring, or certainty that completing a session means improvement. Missing evidence should produce a modest observation or invite reflection. Reflection and feedback choices are independent.

### 9. Save and adapt/repeat

Automatically save session information to a real app-local SESSIONS spreadsheet after each completed or ended run, including when reflection/feedback is declined. Recommended implementation: durable local records plus an automatically maintained `SESSIONS.csv`, readable by spreadsheet applications and by history lookup. CSV is the spreadsheet artifact, not an optional manual export. Keep conservative columns emphasizing topics and quantitative values for mass review and cross-session pattern recognition. Separate detail files hold scene graphs, transcripts, and model metadata.

Recommended columns: session_id, started_at_utc, topic_short, intensity_1_10, planned_seconds, active_seconds, outcome, stop_count, early_end, use_history, chained_rerun, reported_difficulty, reported_readiness, reflection_file, parent_session_id. Update by session_id to prevent duplicates; write atomically and repair from durable local records if interrupted. Escape spreadsheet-formula prefixes in exported user text.

Ask: “Based on your last performance, would you like to go again?” YES starts a chained session at an adapted level; NO returns through the preloader/whoosh to the original forest/START screen. Explain the proposed adaptation briefly and allow correction. Do not add a mandatory difficulty-selection screen.

Adaptation uses the user's self-report, explicit preferences, relevant history, and observed task behavior—not inferred mental health. Maintain, increase, or decrease meaningful scenario dimensions such as audience size, conversational challenge, time pressure, spatial openness, environmental distraction. Preserve the scenario's identity unless the user asks to change it. Explicit requests take priority; high reported difficulty or an early STOP should favor easing or maintaining; missing evidence favors maintaining. Do not treat this illustrative rule as a clinical protocol or claim a calibrated scale. Record before/after dimensions and rationale. Use a single quantitative intensity_1_10 (integer 1–10) in the plan and spreadsheet, alongside optional finer dimensions. This is a product difficulty index, not a clinical measurement. Keep its meaning consistent across reruns and explain changes.

The renderer exposes reusable primitives, licensed individual assets, materials/textures, lighting, fog, spatial audio placement, animation behaviors, and interaction actions. Reusable components are not preset scenes. Generated assets pass through a trusted asset service with format/size checks. Models cannot execute code, load arbitrary URLs, control device permissions, or mutate the camera freely. Enforce scene budgets appropriate to the phone without turning them into topic restrictions. Use coherent stylized rendering as a practical foundation, with expressive characters and visual detail, not a mandate for crude boxes.

## State, dialogue, and scene mechanics

App phases: SETUP, LOBBY, INTAKE, HISTORY_PROMPT, PLANNING, INTRO, REVEAL, ACTIVE, SELF_REPORT, REFLECTION, FEEDBACK_PROMPT, FEEDBACK, SAVING, REPEAT_PROMPT. A preloader transition wrapper covers page changes. Pause is an independent set of reasons so resuming one interruption cannot override another. Track user_stop, portrait, app_inactive, audio_interruption, tracking_unavailable, command_channel_unavailable. Preserve prior phase and media offsets. Returning to landscape clears only portrait; NO to “Quit simulation?” clears only user_stop.

Use a monotonic active clock; wall-clock UTC for storage. Freeze all simulation beats and countdown while paused. Intro/reveal have separate clocks. Stop/cancel invalidate pending responses; late completions cannot start media or resurrect a session. On relaunch, mark unfinished sessions interrupted and return to the lobby. If essential command recognition is unavailable with no working local fallback, pause rather than continue a voice-only session.

Dialogue phases: LISTENING → THINKING → SPEAKING → LISTENING. Characters have generated roles, appearance, goals, context, and behavior boundaries. Use the user's actual utterance and recent history plus a compact summary; generate short contextually responsive replies and supported gestures. Barge-in cancels speech. One speaking character at a time prevents unintelligible overlap; other characters can react visually. STOP overrides dialogue and builder turns. Silence prompts gently instead of inventing an answer. Catch stale turn IDs/revisions before playback. During network delay keep the character in a neutral listening/thinking animation.

Situational actions are generated choices or recognized intents tied to validated operations: inspect/reveal an object, change a property, speak to a character, choose a route, transition viewpoint with fade, or update the scene. Keep a spatial map and stable node IDs so routes and changes remain coherent. Never require physical walking, imply tracked real-world location, or allow a model-generated action to resume/exit the app. Character appearance uses generated component assembly or validated assets; basic idle/listen/speak/gesture animation is reusable, with native TTS voice IDs selected from available voices.

Use the same session_id/revision across specialist outputs. The file is a project proposal to implement, not a claim that providers accept OpenAPI directly. Adapt each provider call to its documented structured-output or tool interface; parse/validate JSON when native schema enforcement is absent.

Coordinator prompt sections: product purpose and nonclinical framing; user input as data; current ability; relevant authorized history; current renderer/audio/action capabilities; required output schema; examples; stable-decision rule; intensity/timer/narration constraints. Specialist prompts contain the approved plan, exact capability/asset manifest, specialist schema, and consistency requirements. Feedback prompts contain only supplied observations and explicit evidence IDs. Treat user speech/history as content, not tool instructions.

Project tool suite: compose_scene, plan_audio, generate_feedback, adapt_session, character_turn, revise_scene. Each carries typed arguments and an execute_when phase, not an empty label. The dispatcher attaches session ID/revision and trusted capability/asset manifests. Feedback/adaptation calls declared during setup are deferred execution instructions for after the session, when their observation sources exist; never fabricate future observations. Reject tool calls in the wrong phase and asset IDs outside the manifest. Safety decisions are enforced before specialist dispatch, not just displayed in model output.

### Scene authoring and pre-commit quality contract

This contract hardens generated practice-scene quality only. It adds no fields, schema variants, or endpoints. The coordinator and Wonder must obey it; the native client validates the assembled scene, approved SessionPlan, resolved assets, and runtime interaction bindings before atomic commit. Schema-valid JSON alone does not establish scene quality. Use the actual viewer profile and renderer capability manifest for geometric and resource checks. The complete shared model prompt instructions are in `scene-authoring-prompts.md`.

1. **Stereographic world.** Require meaningful foreground, midground, and far geometry visible from the initial viewpoint, atmospheric fog, and actual eye separation supplied by the app's calibrated viewer profile. Derive depth layers from composed node transforms and visible geometry; do not add layer fields. Validate depth distribution in both eye renders, rather than accepting three hidden nodes at different distances. `stereo.presentation` remains `stereographic`, `stereo.camera_control` remains `app_owned`. Texture dressing and distant backdrops may supplement the world; a fullscreen image or single quad cannot constitute the practice scene. For confined spaces, express foreground enclosure, midground task, and farther enclosure/visible depth within that setting; do not enlarge the room or invent an outdoor vista just to meet a generic distance threshold. Distance bands and fog must suit the scene's physical scale while preserving the required depth separation.
2. **Visible task focal point.** At least one `nodes[i].role = task_focus` must be meaningfully visible and unoccluded from `initial_viewpoint_id` after reveal. Resolve the viewpoint and node transforms, check both-eye frusta, and use offscreen depth/object-ID rendering or equivalent visibility checks against the actual loaded geometry. A node merely existing or having its center in frame is insufficient. Use a task-appropriate projected area threshold from the renderer profile; do not accept a tiny exposed pixel. Verify after the initial events that affect visibility are applied.
3. **Finished characters and key props.** Compose recognizable, coherent characters/props from validated component assemblies or assets. No empty unlabeled boxes or crude placeholder geometry as final characters/key props. Simple geometry is acceptable as part of a finished assembly or a genuine box-shaped object; primitive type alone is not a quality verdict. Validate asset provenance/capability metadata, assembly completeness, materials, and resolved role/description. Characters must bind to working idle/listen/speak/gesture behaviors; the existing `animation` value selects current behavior, not the complete capability set. Runtime component/asset metadata supplies supported behaviors without extending SceneSpec. Key props need a recognizable task-relevant representation; inanimate props need not speak or gesture. Use pre-commit rendered checks to catch obvious placeholder/empty silhouettes; semantic visual quality also needs authoring judgment rather than a claim that JSON proves it.
4. **Interaction mode is realized.** Read `SessionPlan.interaction_mode`. For `dialogue`, require characters whose `scene_node_id` resolves plus live turn hooks in the app's dialogue controller for those IDs and usable speaking/listening behavior. For `navigation` and `mixed`, require nonempty `interactions[].choices`, unique stable choice IDs, and coherent `viewpoints[].id` destinations connected by supported `viewpoint_transition` operations. A choice that only changes text does not supply navigation. For `mixed`, also validate every dialogue/solo behavior promised by the plan. For `solo`, require task-relevant timed `events` or inspect affordances implemented through choices and supported operations such as reveal/narrate. No mode may commit with zero matching operational interactions. These hooks are app runtime bindings derived from existing plan/scene IDs, not new scene schema fields.
5. **Phone resource limits without task changes.** The app's renderer capability profile sets explicit node, draw-call, geometry, texture/asset-byte, and resident-memory caps for the target phone, including stereo rendering overhead. Provide these caps to the models through the existing capability context; measure actual draw/resource counts after asset resolution and offscreen load. Schema node limits alone are insufficient. On over-budget output, use LOD, instancing, batching, texture/detail reductions, or removal of nonessential decorative props. Preserve topic, `intensity_1_10`, duration, task identity, required characters/choices, and focal visibility; do not remove meaningful crowd pressure or task cues in the name of fewer props. Such optimizations may be deterministic renderer operations without new schema fields. If the scene still cannot fit, return explicit validation errors for the single repair attempt. Never swap a preset room or reduce the task silently.
6. **Consistent references and spatial logic.** Scene `session_id` and `revision` match the approved plan. Referenced nodes, props, characters, sound cues, choices, and viewpoint destinations exist with the correct type. Character IDs join through `SessionPlan.characters`; sound cues resolve against AudioPlan. Route choices describe the actual destination, required destinations are reachable, and viewpoint clearance and orientation remain usable. Preserve stable IDs across targeted revisions when entities retain identity. Stereo camera ownership and calibration cannot be overridden by scene output. Check these after optimizations as well as before them.
7. **Bounded failure path.** Return existing `ValidationIssue` objects with exact document paths, a rule name, and an actionable explanation. Examples: `/nodes/12` for occluded task focus, `/stereo/presentation` for flat rendering, `/interactions/0/choices/1/operations/0/destination_id` for an invalid route, or `/nodes` for draw-budget excess with measured and allowed counts. Refer to related SessionPlan paths in the message when needed. Permit one bounded model repair for the assembled generation attempt, shared with other validation repairs and inside the existing 120-second deadline. Revalidate before commit. If it fails or time expires, offer retry/revise/cancel, with honest status, no fake progress, and no preset substitute. A repair cannot change coordinator task decisions absent new user input.

The coordinator supplies scene depth intent, a focal task, recognizable characters/props, and mode-specific interaction expectations through existing setting/props/character/tool-argument fields. Wonder returns the existing SceneSpec. Both receive the approved plan, capability/asset context, and the shared prompt contract. The client must reject a noncompliant assembled scene even if the model claims it passed. This changes scene authoring and commit validation only; it does not change the engagement loop, STOP behavior, forest lobby, or installation/demo paths.

## Edge cases and weaknesses the implementer must handle

| Case | Expected behavior |
|---|---|
| NSFW fear/request | Distinguish legitimate uncomfortable practice from prohibited sexual or abusive generation. Offer a non-graphic representation preserving legitimate practice intent where possible; explain content limits rather than silently changing the subject. |
| Human interaction | Generate responsive characters and dialogue using actual user answers, including interruption and silence handling. |
| Noticeably malicious intent / prompt injection | User descriptions cannot override system/tool contracts or obtain harmful real-world instructions. Limit generation/actions to the app's simulation capabilities. |
| Clinical request | Keep situational-practice framing, no diagnosis or treatment plan; do not reject ordinary discomfort merely because the user calls it fear. |
| Severe discomfort / STOP | Immediate pause/quit control; no pressure to continue or automatic escalation. |
| Trail / confined-space scenario | Maintain stationary physical use and coherent virtual choices; do not present generated material as authoritative emergency guidance. |
| Mic denied, accent/noise, echo, speaker captures its own STOP prompt | Handle permissions outside viewer; distinguish app playback where possible, use echo cancellation/route tuning, never disable exit monitoring to hide echo problems. Supply backup input. |
| Partial/duplicate/out-of-order transcript | Conservative partial STOP may pause; other commands normally need final utterances. Deduplicate utterance/event IDs and state-check yes/no. |
| Word “stop” occurs within normal dialogue | Prefer an unnecessary pause over missed stop; make NO resume easy. Test the reminder itself does not trigger an endless pause loop. |
| Repeated hey builder / stale output | Newest revision wins, preserve all accepted additions, discard stale replies/assets atomically. |
| Intro too long | Condense and synthesize again to fit eight seconds including STOP; never truncate essential instruction. |
| Portrait while already STOP-paused | Returning landscape does not dismiss confirmation; NO while portrait keeps portrait pause. |
| Phone call/background/lock/socket loss | Pause/mute; restore resources, then resume only when the remaining interruption conditions are resolved. |
| Bluetooth route change / narration overlap | Reconfigure audio safely, preserve speech position, keep commands usable. |
| Scene invalid, assets missing, model invents capability | Repair with explicit errors or explain/retry; never execute model code or replace with a preset. |
| Too many objects, heat, frame drops | Reduce rendering cost without changing task intent; pause if the experience becomes unusable. |
| Incoherent room, occluded focal point, route contradiction | Spatial/semantic checks and targeted regeneration using stable IDs. Schema validity alone is insufficient. |
| Two-minute goal missed / provider rate limit | Honest status, bounded retry respecting Retry-After within the 120-second attempt ceiling, retry/revise/cancel choice; never fake progress or a live generation result. |
| Missing evidence / self-report contradicts telemetry | Label sources, prioritize explicit preferences; no invented feedback or false precision in adaptation. |
| Storage full, crash during CSV write, duplicated save | Preserve durable minimal record, atomic CSV repair, visible save status, idempotent session ID. |
| Reflection naming/path collision | <=30-character topic, sanitized disk/export name, retain display title and disambiguate collision. |
| Privacy | Disclose remote transcription/model processing, minimize logs, no raw audio storage by default, save optional reflection only when chosen; support deleting local records/files and repairing CSV. |
| Demo network outage | Retry or explicitly replay a previously generated session; label replay honestly. |

Main weaknesses to solve in implementation: convincing generated spatial composition, coherent interactive characters, stereo optics on the actual viewer, speech latency/echo, and the one-minute generation goal. None is solved by declaring a schema. Work on real output/device behavior immediately rather than building a large abstraction layer first.

## Desired example cases—not preset choices

- Community-garden pitch to eight neighbors in a sunny library: relevant audience, usable speaking area, responsive questions.
- Project update to three colleagues in an industrial studio: visibly distinct layout/materials and interaction.
- Wedding toast beneath a garden canopy at dusk: outdoor depth, tables, warm lights, contextual audience reactions.
- Design-role interview in a busy studio: questions grounded in the role and follow-ups reacting to actual answers.
- Lost trail near dusk: coherent landmarks and voice-driven inspection/route decisions while physically stationary.
- Small elevator stopped between floors: enclosed geometry, relevant audio, composure/problem-solving task, app STOP independent of fictional doors.
- “Make that elevator less enclosed but keep the same place”: preserve identity while revising spatial dimensions and lighting.
- “Same garden toast, fewer people, brighter light”: targeted revision, not unrelated regeneration.
- An exceptionally large crowd: use rendering techniques such as instancing/level of detail where possible; explain a genuine device limit and propose a faithful approximation if necessary.
